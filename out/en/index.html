02The craft

Cut by hand.Stitched point by point.Burnished until the edge takes the light.

There is no sewing machine in this workshop. Each hole is marked with an awl and crossed with two needles, one after the other. A bag is six to nine hours on the bench. It is slower, and it is the only way we know a seam will still hold in twenty years.

Medellín, Colombia

Full grain · Antioquia

03The hide

Four things
come through
the door

Nothing else. No synthetic linings, no glue in place of a stitch, no plated hardware that flakes in two years.

Full-grain hide
Antioquia
The outer face of the skin, grain left intact. It is not sanded or corrected: the scars and marks of the animal stay where they are.
Vegetable tan
Acacia and quebracho
Forty-five days in the pit instead of two in the drum. It is slow, it is costly, and it is why the leather darkens instead of cracking.
Waxed linen thread
0.45 and 0.55 mm
The hide will give before the seam does. And if one stitch fails, the saddle stitch keeps the rest of the row from running.
Solid brass
Cast by hand
No plating. It darkens with the years and can be brought back to a shine. Chromed hardware, once it flakes, is finished.

04How it is made

Six steps, always in the same order

Days pass between the first and the last, not hours. The hide rests between steps: if it is forced, it marks.

  1. 01

    Selection

    The part of the hide is chosen for what it will have to hold. The back for what is pulled taut; the belly for what must fold.

  2. 02

    Cutting

    Round knife and a cardboard pattern. One pass along the edge: a second pass always shows on the finish.

  3. 03

    Edges

    The arris is taken down, sanded through three grits, and the edge is dampened before anything else touches it.

  4. 04

    Stitching

    Saddle stitch, two needles, waxed linen. One hole at a time, marked first with the awl.

  5. 05

    Burnishing

    Wood and friction until the edge stops taking water and starts giving back the light.

  6. 06

    Finish

    Animal fat, a day of rest, and a last check against the light, stitch by stitch.

05Time

The leather changes with you

Vegetable-tanned, with no pigment to cover it. It leaves the workshop pale and darkens with light, the oil of the hands and the rub of the days. The three pieces below are the same purse: new, at one year, at ten.

  1. 01 · Day one

    It leaves pale, almost raw.

  2. 02 · Year one

    The honey starts to show.

  3. 03 · Year ten

    Dark amber, the grain in plain sight.

06The workshop

Tools older than the workshop

Almost all of them are second-hand. The round knife arrived without a handle and cuts better than anything sold today. They are sharpened every morning, before the hide is touched.
